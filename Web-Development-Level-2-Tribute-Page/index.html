His Life

About Nelson Mandela

Nelson Rolihlahla Mandela was born on 18 July 1918 in Mvezo, South Africa. He became a central figure in the struggle against apartheid, a system of racial segregation and discrimination in South Africa.

Mandela studied law and joined the African National Congress in 1944. His work against apartheid led to his imprisonment for more than 27 years.

On 11 February 1990, he was released from prison. In 1993, he received the Nobel Peace Prize jointly with F. W. de Klerk. On 10 May 1994, Mandela was inaugurated as South Africa's first democratically elected president.

He served as president until 1999. Nelson Mandela passed away on 5 December 2013, leaving a lasting historical legacy.

His Influence

A Lasting Legacy

Nelson Mandela's life remains an important part of South African and world history. His journey continues to be studied in connection with human rights, democracy, reconciliation, and the struggle against racial discrimination.

His legacy is remembered through educational programs, the work of the Nelson Mandela Foundation, and the annual Nelson Mandela International Day on 18 July.
